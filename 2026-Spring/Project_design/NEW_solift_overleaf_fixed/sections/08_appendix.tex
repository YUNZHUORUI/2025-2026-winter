\section*{Appendix A: Propeller Thrust Calculation -- Detailed Derivation}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendix A: Propeller Thrust Calculation}
%---------------------------------------------------------------

The hover thrust required per front motor is derived from momentum theory. At MTOW:

\begin{equation}
    T_{required} = \frac{m_{MTOW} \cdot g}{N_{motors}} = \frac{12.0 \times 9.81}{2} = \SI{58.9}{N} = \SI{6.0}{kgf}
\end{equation}

\noindent with a design margin of 50\% (thrust-to-weight = 1.8:1 total), each motor must produce:
\begin{equation}
    T_{motor} = \frac{12.0 \times 9.81 \times 1.8}{2} = \SI{105.9}{N} \approx \SI{10.8}{kgf}
\end{equation}

\noindent From actuator disk theory, the induced velocity in hover is:
\begin{equation}
    v_i = \sqrt{\frac{T}{2 \rho A_{disk}}}
\end{equation}
where $A_{disk} = \pi \left(\frac{D_{prop}}{2}\right)^2 = \pi \times (0.279)^2 = \SI{0.245}{m^2}$ for the 22-inch (0.559~m diameter) propeller, and $\rho = \SI{1.225}{kg/m^3}$ at sea level:
\begin{equation}
    v_i = \sqrt{\frac{58.9}{2 \times 1.225 \times 0.245}} = \sqrt{98.2} = \SI{9.91}{m/s}
\end{equation}

\noindent Power required per motor in hover:
\begin{equation}
    P_{hover} = \frac{T \cdot v_i}{\eta_{motor}} = \frac{58.9 \times 9.91}{0.85} = \SI{686}{W}
\end{equation}

\noindent The Sunnysky X4112S is rated at $\sim$700~W continuous at 12S, confirming the motor selection is appropriate with minimal margin for thermal management requirements.

\section*{Appendix B: Solar Energy Budget Calculation}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendix B: Solar Energy Budget}
%---------------------------------------------------------------

\noindent The solar energy contribution to cruise range is calculated as follows.

\noindent Available solar power in cruise:
\begin{equation}
    P_{solar} = G \cdot A_{PV} \cdot \eta_{PV} = 1000 \times 0.95 \times 0.245 = \SI{232.8}{W}
\end{equation}

\noindent Cruise power required (total, at 18~m/s, 5~kg payload):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    P_{cruise} &= \frac{D \cdot V}{1000} = \frac{(m \cdot g / (L/D)) \cdot V}{1000} \\
               &= \frac{(12.0 \times 9.81 / 45.6) \times 18}{1000} = \SI{46.6}{W} \text{ (aerodynamic only)}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent Adding propulsion efficiency losses ($\eta = 0.75$) and avionics ($\sim$30~W):
\begin{equation}
    P_{total} = \frac{46.6}{0.75} + 30 = \SI{92.1}{W}
\end{equation}

\noindent Net battery drain rate with solar:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{E}_{net} = P_{total} - P_{solar} = 92.1 - 232.8 \rightarrow \text{Net charge: solar exceeds cruise demand}
\end{equation}

\noindent This confirms that under STC illumination, the solar array alone can sustain cruise flight. The battery serves as a buffer for VTOL phases and cloudy-day operation.

\noindent Estimated range (battery only, 5~kg payload):
\begin{equation}
    R_{batt} = \frac{E_{battery}}{P_{total}} \times V_\infty = \frac{977 \times 3600}{92.1} \times 18 \approx \SI{68}{km}
\end{equation}
